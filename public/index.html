T = (C + R + I) / S

The Trust Equation

Three things that help, divided by one that hurts.

  • Trustworthiness T

    The whole. How far one person can rely on another, as judged by that person. It is never a fixed trait: someone scores it, about someone.

    Three things that help, divided by one that hurts. The divisor is the point: a small rise in self-orientation can undo a lot of credibility.

  • Credibility C

    Words. Can I believe what they say? Do they know their subject, and do they say "I don't know" when they don't?

    Shows up as expertise, a track record, honesty, precision, and admitting the limits of what they know. Lowered by exaggeration, bluffing, and name-dropping.

    "I can trust what they say about this."

  • Reliability R

    Actions. Can I depend on them? Do they do what they said, when they said?

    Shows up as kept promises, met deadlines, steady moods, and an early warning when something slips. Built slowly through repetition. Lowered by surprises.

    "I can trust them to do what they said."

  • Intimacy I

    Safety. Can I tell them something that matters without it coming back to bite me?

    Shows up as discretion, empathy, candour, and being at ease with difficult subjects. Nothing to do with physical closeness. Lowered by gossip, judgement, and using what you shared.

    "I can trust them with something that matters to me."

  • Self-orientation S

    Focus. How much of their attention is on themselves rather than on you? This one divides. High self-orientation undoes everything above it.

    Shows up as listening in order to reply, me-talk, needing to be right, steering toward their own gain, and worrying how they come across. The authors say "self-obsession" may be the better word. Low self-orientation looks like curiosity about you.

    "I can trust that they care about me, not just about looking good."

Each factor is scored 1 to 10, and the result runs from 0.3 to 30. Credibility, reliability and intimacy are scored high-is-good. Self-orientation runs the other way: 1 means their focus is all on you, 10 means it is all on them.

The Trust Equation comes from The Trusted Advisor (Maister, Green & Galford, 2000). trustedadvisor.com/equation
